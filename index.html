Staff Data Scientist, LATAM Airlines · independent researcher

I study how to turn data into decisions that hold up once they are live. My work combines causal inference, decision science, optimization and machine learning, and I apply it in two places: quantitative finance (change-point detection, regime-switching simulation, return forecasting) and large-scale recommendation and personalization.

The common thread is evaluation. A model is only useful if its estimates survive leakage, non-stationarity and the feedback loop created by acting on them. I try to measure that before trusting any gain.

Selected work

Real-time structural-break detection with synthetic worlds

Competition · write-up
ADIA Lab Structural Break Challenge, Real-Time Edition (CrunchDAO) · 2026

A causal detector that, at every step, scores how likely a series has already broken. The final system is a per-step weighted vote of five members: CatBoost on 132 causal features, two temporal convolutional networks, and two students distilled from TabPFN. Training data for the networks is augmented with synthetic series generated from the training set itself, using domain randomization and MS-GARCH regime-pair resampling.

What I learned
  • The same synthetic data helped the neural members and hurt CatBoost in every variant tried.
  • A standalone gain from synthetic data said almost nothing about the gain inside the blend, in either direction.
  • Measuring the implementation noise floor (thread count alone moved a CatBoost ablation by about 0.003) changed which ablations I considered informative.

Two-level residual models for airport taxi-out prediction

Competition · code
PRC Data Challenge 2026 (EUROCONTROL / OpenSky) · ten European airports

Prediction of departure taxi-out time. Level 1 blends two gradient-boosting bases fitted on the residual against the schedule clock. Level 2 stacks residual layers on top: an ADS-B surface corrector built from open tracks, airport-specific rule and model layers, per-airport ridge/lasso combiners with neural members, a daily posterior update that uses published delay totals as a constraint, and a runway-regime layer.

Geometry-Aware Flight-Time Prediction from Convective Polygon Intersections

Manuscript in preparation
Bruno Vilela Quirino et al. · from the LATAM/ITA/DECEA challenge (2nd place, 2023)

Treats convective weather as geometry: storm imagery is segmented into georeferenced polygons and spatially joined to radar-derived flight tracks, instead of collapsing weather into airport-level scalars.

Method and result
  • Fuses movement logs, EUROCONTROL CAT-62 radar tracks, METAR/METAF reports and image-derived polygons in one LightGBM pipeline.
  • 286,080 flights, 73 engineered predictors; 5-fold CV RMSE 363 s (473 s held out), roughly halving the error on convective flights against a no-imagery baseline.
